What Is Sciatica?

Sciatica is not a diagnosis on its own. It is a symptom caused by irritation or compression of the sciatic nerve, the longest and thickest nerve in the human body. The sciatic nerve starts in the lower back, travels through the buttock, runs down the back of each leg, and branches into the foot. When this nerve is compressed or inflamed, pain, numbness, tingling, or weakness can radiate along its entire path.

Most cases of sciatica are caused by a structural problem in the lower spine, most commonly a herniated disc (slipped disc) pressing on the nerve root. Other causes include spinal stenosis, degenerative disc disease, spondylolisthesis, and piriformis syndrome. Accurate diagnosis of the underlying cause is essential before any treatment can be effective. This is precisely why choosing the right sciatica specialist doctor in Hyderabad matters so much.

Sciatica Symptoms You Should Not Ignore

Many patients delay getting help because they hope the pain will resolve on its own. While mild sciatica sometimes settles with rest, persistent or worsening symptoms require prompt evaluation by a qualified sciatica doctor in Hyderabad. Do not delay if you are experiencing any of the following.

  • Shooting leg pain: Shooting pain that starts in the lower back and travels down one leg is the defining feature of sciatica. The pain can range from a dull ache to a sharp, burning sensation that makes walking extremely difficult.
  • Burning or tingling sensation: A constant burning or tingling sensation in the leg or foot is a common presentation of nerve irritation.
  • Numbness or loss of sensation: Numbness or loss of sensation along the back of the leg may indicate ongoing nerve compression and should be evaluated.
  • Weakness in the leg or foot: Weakness affecting walking, standing up, or climbing stairs suggests more significant nerve involvement and requires prompt attention.
  • Pain worsened by sitting or standing: Pain that worsens significantly when sitting or standing for extended periods often indicates nerve root compression.
  • Red flags needing urgent care: Sudden loss of bladder or bowel control, progressive weakness in both legs, and severe pain following a fall or trauma are all indicators that immediate evaluation by a sciatica specialist is necessary.

Common Causes of Sciatica

Understanding why sciatica develops is essential to treating it effectively and preventing it from returning. The most frequent causes seen at our sciatica pain treatment centre in Hyderabad include the following.

  • Lumbar Disc Herniation: This is the single most common cause of sciatica. When the soft inner material of a spinal disc pushes through its outer casing, it can press directly on the nearby sciatic nerve root, causing immediate and severe nerve pain.
  • Lumbar Spinal Stenosis: Narrowing of the spinal canal, particularly in older patients, places pressure on the spinal cord and nerve roots, leading to sciatica that typically worsens with walking and improves with sitting or bending forward.
  • Spondylolisthesis: When a vertebra slips forward over the one below it, the resulting spinal instability can compress the sciatic nerve. This condition is seen in both younger athletic patients and older adults with degenerative spinal changes.
  • Degenerative Disc Disease: As spinal discs lose height and hydration with age, the foramen (the opening through which nerve roots exit the spine) can narrow, trapping and irritating the sciatic nerve.
  • Piriformis Syndrome: In some patients, the piriformis muscle in the buttock irritates the sciatic nerve as it passes beneath or through it. This is a less common but frequently overlooked cause of sciatica-like symptoms.
  • Spinal Tumours or Infections: Though rare, abnormal growths or spinal infections can compress the sciatic nerve and require urgent assessment.

Sciatica Pain Treatment Options

Our approach to sciatica pain treatment in Hyderabad covers the full spectrum from conservative non-surgical therapies to precision surgical intervention, depending entirely on the underlying cause and severity of each patient's condition.

Non-Surgical Sciatica Treatment

The majority of sciatica cases resolve with structured, well-managed non-surgical care. Our non-surgical spine treatment programme includes several evidence-based approaches that consistently deliver excellent outcomes.

  • Targeted Physiotherapy: A customised physiotherapy programme addresses the root mechanical cause of sciatic nerve irritation. Core strengthening, spinal mobilisation, nerve gliding exercises, and postural correction work together to decompress the sciatic nerve and prevent recurrence.
  • Epidural Steroid Injections: When nerve inflammation is severe, an injection of corticosteroid directly into the epidural space around the affected nerve root dramatically reduces swelling and provides rapid, sustained pain relief. All injections are performed under image guidance for precision.
  • Nerve Root Blocks: A targeted nerve root block delivers anaesthetic and anti-inflammatory medication directly to the compressed nerve root. This provides immediate relief while the underlying condition is managed.
  • Extracorporeal Shock Wave Therapy (ESWT): For chronic sciatic pain involving soft tissue and muscle components, ESWT uses focused acoustic energy to stimulate healing at the tissue level, reduce pain, and improve mobility.
  • Ultrasound-Guided Injections: Our facility uses real-time ultrasound guidance for all injection procedures. This ensures medications reach the exact target structure every time, maximising results and minimising risk.
  • Medication Management: Anti-inflammatory medications, muscle relaxants, and neuropathic pain agents are prescribed as short-term adjuncts to other therapies. They are never offered as a standalone solution.
    For patients whose sciatic symptoms are connected to generalised musculoskeletal pain or arthritis, our pain management team and alternative treatments for knee arthritis and back problems service offer complementary care pathways.

Surgical Sciatica Treatment

When non-surgical care does not deliver adequate relief over an appropriate period, or when a patient presents with significant nerve deficit, weakness, or bowel and bladder involvement, surgery becomes the correct and most effective option. Our spine surgical treatment team is experienced in all procedures relevant to sciatica.

  • Microdiscectomy: This is the gold-standard surgical treatment for sciatica caused by a lumbar disc herniation. Through a small incision in the lower back, the portion of the herniated disc pressing on the sciatic nerve is precisely removed. The procedure takes approximately 45 to 90 minutes, most patients are walking the same day, and the majority return home within 24 to 48 hours of surgery. Success rates for well-selected patients are consistently above 90 percent.
  • Laminotomy or Laminectomy: For sciatica caused by spinal stenosis, a laminotomy removes a small portion of the lamina to create more space in the spinal canal and relieve pressure on the nerve. A full laminectomy is performed for more extensive stenosis.
  • Spinal Fusion: In cases where sciatic pain is caused by spondylolisthesis or significant spinal instability, fusion surgery permanently stabilises the affected vertebral segment using bone grafts and implants.

All surgical procedures at this facility are supported by Intraoperative Neurophysiological Monitoring (IONM). An overview of the comprehensive spine care approach is available on our best spine surgeon in Hyderabad page.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does sciatica take to recover?

Mild to moderate sciatica managed with non-surgical care typically improves significantly within four to eight weeks of starting a structured treatment programme. Some patients see relief within days of receiving targeted injections. Patients who require surgery typically return to full activity within six to twelve weeks of their procedure.

Can sciatica go away without treatment?

Very mild acute sciatica sometimes resolves with rest and time. However, persistent, severe, or recurring sciatica rarely resolves completely without proper treatment. Delayed treatment can allow nerve damage to progress. If your symptoms have lasted more than two to three weeks or are getting worse, see a sciatica specialist without delay.

Is surgery the only option for sciatica?

No. The majority of sciatica patients are successfully treated without surgery. Physiotherapy, injections, and other non-surgical approaches deliver excellent results for most patients. Surgery is recommended only when conservative care has been thoroughly explored and has not delivered adequate relief, or when nerve damage is progressing.

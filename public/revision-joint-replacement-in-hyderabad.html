Revision Knee and Hip Replacement Surgery in Hyderabad

A knee or hip replacement that once worked well can, over many years, start to loosen, wear down or become infected. When this happens, revision joint replacement, a second surgery to repair or replace the original implant, becomes necessary. Revision surgery is more complex than a first-time replacement and calls for a surgical team experienced in managing bone loss and altered anatomy from the earlier procedure.

What Is Revision Joint Replacement?

Revision joint replacement involves removing some or all of a previously implanted knee or hip replacement and replacing it with new components. Depending on the extent of the problem, this may involve specialized implants designed to make up for lost bone, longer stems for added stability, or, in cases of infection, a staged approach where an antibiotic spacer is placed before the final implant is fitted.

Signs Your Knee or Hip Replacement May Be Failing

  • Increasing pain in a joint that had been pain-free since the original surgery
  • New swelling, warmth or redness around the joint
  • A feeling of instability, as though the joint might give way
  • Clicking, grinding or a change in how the joint sounds or feels during movement
  • Difficulty bearing weight or a noticeable change in walking pattern
  • Persistent low-grade fever alongside joint symptoms, which can point to infection

Any of these symptoms in a patient with an existing knee or hip replacement deserves prompt evaluation.

Common Reasons Implants Need Revision

Modern joint implants are built to last for decades, and long-term studies show implant survival rates above 95 percent at ten years and roughly 85 to 90 percent at twenty years for knee and hip replacements. This durability applies to both total knee replacement and total hip replacement.

Revision becomes necessary in the smaller group of cases affected by:

  • Aseptic loosening, where the implant gradually separates from the bone without infection
  • Wear of the plastic liner between implant components over many years of use
  • Infection around the implant, which may occur soon after surgery or years later
  • Instability or dislocation of the joint
  • A fracture of the bone around the implant, often after a fall
  • Malalignment from the original surgery that places uneven stress on the joint

Why Revision Surgery Requires Specialized Expertise

Revision surgery is technically more demanding than a first-time joint replacement. The surgeon has to work around scar tissue, assess how much bone has been lost, and often use specialized augmented or custom implants to rebuild a stable joint. Germanten's approach to revision cases draws on international collaboration with Dr. Roland Wetzel, honorary department head for hip and knee primary and revision joint replacement surgery at University Hospital Munich, whose focus on revision cases informs the protocols used for complex cases in Hyderabad.

The Revision Surgery Procedure and Recovery

Revision procedures generally take longer than primary joint replacement, often two to four hours depending on complexity, and may require a longer hospital stay to monitor healing and manage infection risk where relevant. The precision offered by robotic joint replacement backed by German precision supports accurate implant positioning even in these more difficult cases. Recovery is typically slower and more gradual than after a first replacement, with physiotherapy adjusted to the specific bone and soft tissue work involved in that patient's surgery.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: How do I know if my knee or hip replacement is failing?

A: Increasing pain, swelling, instability or a change in how the joint feels during movement in a previously pain-free replacement are the main warning signs, and any of these should prompt a specialist evaluation with imaging.

Q: How long do revision knee or hip implants last?

A: Revision implants are generally durable, though survival rates tend to be somewhat lower than for a first-time replacement due to the more complex starting conditions. Your surgeon can give you a realistic picture based on your specific case.

Q: Is revision surgery riskier than the first joint replacement?

A: Revision surgery is more technically complex and can carry a higher risk profile than a primary replacement, which is why it should be performed by a surgeon experienced specifically in revision cases.

Q: What causes a joint replacement to fail years after surgery?

A: The most common causes are gradual loosening of the implant, wear of the plastic component, infection, instability or a fracture around the implant, usually after many years of otherwise normal use.

Q: How long does recovery take after revision joint replacement?

A: Recovery is generally slower than after a first replacement and depends heavily on how much bone and soft tissue work was needed during surgery. Your physiotherapy team will set a personalized timeline based on your procedure.

Revision Knee and Hip Replacement Surgery in Hyderabad

A knee or hip replacement that once worked well can, over many years, start to loosen, wear down or become infected.

When this happens, revision joint replacement, a second surgery to repair or replace the original implant, may become necessary.

Revision surgery is more complex than a first-time replacement and requires a surgical team experienced in managing bone loss and altered anatomy from the earlier procedure.

What Is Revision Joint Replacement?

Revision joint replacement involves removing some or all of a previously implanted knee or hip replacement and replacing it with new components.

Depending on the extent of the problem, this may involve specialised implants designed to compensate for lost bone, longer stems for added stability, or, in cases of infection, a staged approach in which an antibiotic spacer is placed before the final implant is fitted.

Signs Your Knee or Hip Replacement May Be Failing

  • Increasing pain in a joint that had been pain-free since the original surgery
  • New swelling, warmth or redness around the joint
  • A feeling of instability, as though the joint might give way
  • Clicking, grinding or a change in how the joint sounds or feels during movement
  • Difficulty bearing weight or a noticeable change in walking pattern
  • Persistent low-grade fever alongside joint symptoms, which can point to infection

Any of these symptoms in a patient with an existing knee or hip replacement deserves prompt specialist evaluation.

Common Reasons Implants Need Revision

Modern joint implants are built to last for decades. Long-term studies report implant survival rates above 95 percent at ten years and approximately 85 to 90 percent at twenty years for knee and hip replacements.

Revision becomes necessary in the smaller group of cases affected by:

  • Aseptic loosening: The implant gradually separates from the bone without infection
  • Plastic liner wear: The plastic component between implant parts wears down after many years of use
  • Infection: Infection develops around the implant soon after surgery or several years later
  • Instability or dislocation: The joint becomes unstable or moves out of position
  • Periprosthetic fracture: The bone around the implant fractures, often after a fall
  • Malalignment: Uneven alignment from the original surgery places abnormal stress on the joint

Why Revision Surgery Requires Specialised Expertise

Revision surgery is technically more demanding than a first-time joint replacement.

The surgeon must work around scar tissue, assess how much bone has been lost, and often use specialised augmented or custom implants to rebuild a stable joint.

The Revision Surgery Procedure and Recovery

Revision procedures generally take longer than primary joint replacement, often between two and four hours depending on the complexity of the case.

Patients may require a longer hospital stay so the surgical team can monitor healing and manage infection risk where relevant.

Recovery is usually slower and more gradual than after a first replacement. Physiotherapy is adjusted to the specific bone and soft-tissue reconstruction performed during that patient's surgery.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I know if my knee or hip replacement is failing?

Increasing pain, swelling, instability or a change in how the joint feels during movement in a previously pain-free replacement are the main warning signs. Any of these symptoms should prompt specialist evaluation and imaging.

How long do revision knee or hip implants last?

Revision implants are generally durable, although survival rates can be somewhat lower than those of a first-time replacement because the starting conditions are more complex. Your surgeon can provide a realistic outlook based on your case.

Is revision surgery riskier than the first joint replacement?

Revision surgery is more technically complex and may carry a higher risk profile than primary replacement. It should therefore be performed by a surgeon with specific experience in revision procedures.

What causes a joint replacement to fail years after surgery?

Common causes include gradual loosening of the implant, wear of the plastic component, infection, instability or a fracture around the implant, usually after many years of otherwise normal use.

How long does recovery take after revision joint replacement?

Recovery is generally slower than after a first replacement and depends heavily on how much bone and soft-tissue reconstruction was required. The physiotherapy team will provide a personalised timeline based on the procedure.

Procedure Details

What Is Total Hip Replacement?

Total hip replacement, or total hip arthroplasty, replaces the damaged ball of the thighbone and the worn socket of the pelvis with an artificial joint. The new joint is designed to move smoothly through the same range of motion as a healthy hip, allowing patients to walk, sit and climb stairs without the grinding pain that comes from bone rubbing against bone. It is one of the most common and successful forms of joint replacement performed today.

Medical Conditions

Conditions That May Require Hip Replacement

Several conditions can damage the hip joint enough to need replacement surgery:

  • Osteoarthritis: the gradual wearing down of cartilage that comes with age and long-term joint stress
  • Avascular necrosis: where the blood supply to the head of the thighbone is disrupted and the bone tissue begins to collapse, often linked to prolonged steroid use, heavy alcohol use or unknown causes
  • Rheumatoid arthritis and other inflammatory joint conditions
  • Fractures of the hip or thighbone, particularly in older adults after a fall
  • Childhood hip conditions that lead to early joint degeneration in adulthood
Warning Signs

Signs You Should See a Hip Specialist

Persistent groin or thigh pain, pain that spreads to the knee, a noticeable limp, difficulty putting on shoes or socks, and hip stiffness after sitting for long periods are all reasons to consult a hip replacement doctor in Hyderabad for a proper evaluation. Waiting too long can make the surrounding muscles weaker and the eventual recovery slower, so early assessment by an experienced hip replacement surgeon in Hyderabad gives you the widest range of options.

Robotic and Computer-Navigated Hip Replacement

Positioning the hip implant at the correct angle and depth has a direct effect on leg length, joint stability and how naturally the hip moves afterward. Computer navigation and robotic joint replacement backed by German precision allow the surgical team to map these measurements before surgery and verify implant position in real time during the procedure, reducing the guesswork involved in manual technique.

Minimally invasive approaches are used where appropriate to limit muscle disruption, which supports faster early mobilization after surgery.

The Hip Replacement Procedure and Hospital Stay

Hip replacement surgery generally takes one to two hours under spinal or general anesthesia. Patients are encouraged to stand and begin walking with support soon after surgery, often the same day, under the guidance of the physiotherapy team. A typical hospital stay ranges from three to five days, depending on age, general health and how quickly early mobility targets are met.

Recovery After Hip Replacement

The first two weeks focus on wound healing, pain control and learning safe movement patterns to protect the new joint, including precautions around bending and crossing the legs. Walking distance increases steadily over the following weeks with the help of a walker or stick.

By six to eight weeks, most patients have moved on to a cane or are walking unaided for short distances. Full recovery, including a return to normal daily activity and most forms of low-impact exercise, typically takes three to six months.

Long-Term Outcomes of Hip Replacement

Hip replacement has one of the strongest long-term safety records in orthopedic surgery. A population based cohort study following patients for up to twenty years, published via The Lancet, recorded implant survival of over 95 percent at ten years and 85 percent at twenty years. For patients over 70, the lifetime risk of ever needing revision hip replacement was found to be around 5 percent, underlining how durable modern hip implants have become.

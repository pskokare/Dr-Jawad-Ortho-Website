Shoulder Replacement Surgery in Hyderabad

Severe shoulder arthritis or a complex fracture can make even simple movements, including reaching overhead, dressing or lifting a cup, painful and difficult.

When the shoulder joint is damaged beyond what physiotherapy and medication can manage, shoulder replacement surgery can restore movement and relieve pain.

Dr. Mir Jawad Zar Khan evaluates each shoulder case individually to recommend the surgical approach best suited to the extent of damage.

What Is Shoulder Replacement Surgery?

Shoulder replacement surgery replaces the damaged ball and socket of the shoulder joint with an artificial implant.

It is recommended when arthritis, a severe fracture or long-standing rotator cuff damage has worn down the joint surfaces enough to cause constant pain and limited movement that no longer responds to conservative treatment.

Types of Shoulder Replacement

Anatomical Total Shoulder Replacement

Anatomical total shoulder replacement recreates the shoulder's natural ball and socket structure. It is used when the rotator cuff tendons are still intact and functioning.

Reverse Total Shoulder Replacement

Reverse total shoulder replacement switches the position of the ball and socket, allowing the deltoid muscle to take over movement that a damaged rotator cuff can no longer manage.

This approach is often used for patients with rotator cuff tear arthropathy or complex fractures where the rotator cuff cannot be repaired.

Conditions That May Require Shoulder Replacement

  • Severe osteoarthritis or rheumatoid arthritis of the shoulder
  • Rotator cuff tear arthropathy, where a long-standing rotator cuff tear has caused joint damage
  • Complex fractures of the upper arm bone that cannot be reliably fixed using plates or screws
  • Avascular necrosis of the humeral head
  • Failed previous shoulder surgery

Non-Surgical Treatment Is Tried First

Shoulder replacement is considered only after physiotherapy, activity modification, anti-inflammatory medication and, in some cases, injections have failed to provide lasting relief.

A thorough clinical evaluation and imaging confirm the extent of joint damage before surgery is recommended.

The Shoulder Replacement Procedure

Shoulder replacement surgery typically takes around two hours under general or regional anesthesia.

The damaged joint surfaces are removed and replaced with implant components sized to match the patient's own anatomy.

Most patients stay in hospital for two to four days following surgery.

Recovery and Physiotherapy After Shoulder Replacement

A structured physiotherapy programme begins soon after surgery, starting with gentle passive movement to protect the healing tissue.

Treatment progresses to active strengthening exercises over several weeks.

Most patients regain functional use of the arm for daily activities within six to twelve weeks, with continued improvement in strength for several months afterward.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between anatomical and reverse shoulder replacement?

Anatomical replacement recreates the shoulder's natural structure and requires an intact rotator cuff to function well. Reverse replacement changes the joint mechanics so the deltoid muscle can power arm movement, making it suitable when the rotator cuff is severely damaged or torn.

How painful is recovery after shoulder replacement?

Pain is managed with a structured post-operative plan and generally eases significantly during the first few weeks. Some discomfort during physiotherapy exercises is normal as movement is restored.

How long does it take to lift my arm normally after shoulder replacement?

Functional movement for daily tasks usually returns within six to twelve weeks, although full strength and overhead movement can continue improving for up to one year.

Can shoulder replacement fix a rotator cuff tear?

Reverse shoulder replacement is specifically designed to restore function when the rotator cuff cannot be repaired by changing how the shoulder muscles move the joint.

Is shoulder replacement as common as knee or hip replacement?

Shoulder replacement is performed less frequently than knee or hip replacement, which makes surgical experience and correct implant selection especially important for achieving a good outcome.

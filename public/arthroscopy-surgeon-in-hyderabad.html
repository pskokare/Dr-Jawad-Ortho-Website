What Is Arthroscopy?

Arthroscopy is a minimally invasive surgical technique in which a tiny camera, called an arthroscope, is inserted into a joint through a small incision. The camera projects a magnified view of the inside of the joint onto a screen, allowing the surgeon to diagnose the problem precisely and repair it using fine instruments through other small incisions.

Because it avoids the large incisions of open surgery, arthroscopy treatment means less tissue damage, less pain, a lower risk of infection and a significantly faster return to normal activity. It is used both to confirm a diagnosis and to carry out the repair in the same procedure, most commonly on the knee and shoulder.

Meniscus Repair

Keyhole repair or trimming of a torn meniscus, including bucket-handle tears, to relieve pain and locking and preserve the natural cushion of the knee.

Removal of Loose Bodies

Keyhole removal of loose fragments of bone or cartilage and cysts that cause pain and catching in the joint.

Benefits of Arthroscopic Surgery

Minimally Invasive

Small keyhole incisions instead of a large open cut mean less damage to the healthy tissue around the joint.

Less Pain and Scarring

Reduced tissue trauma means significantly less post-operative pain and minimal scarring.

Faster Recovery

Most patients return home the same day or the next, and return to normal activity far sooner than after open surgery.

Lower Infection Risk

Smaller incisions and shorter surgery reduce the risk of infection and complications.

Accurate Diagnosis and Repair

The magnified camera view lets the surgeon both confirm the exact problem and repair it in a single procedure.

Recovery and Pain After Arthroscopy

One of the biggest advantages of keyhole surgery is how manageable the recovery is. Any arthroscopy pain after surgery is generally mild compared with open surgery and is controlled with a structured pain relief plan. Most patients are walking soon after a knee arthroscopy, and a guided physiotherapy programme then rebuilds strength and movement. Recovery time depends on the procedure, from a couple of weeks for a simple meniscus trim to several months of structured rehabilitation for an ACL reconstruction, with the goal always being a safe return to full activity.

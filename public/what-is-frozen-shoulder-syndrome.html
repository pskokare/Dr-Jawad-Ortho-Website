What Is Frozen Shoulder Syndrome?

Frozen shoulder syndrome is a condition where the capsule around the shoulder joint becomes tight, thick, and inflamed. The shoulder joint normally allows smooth movement in different directions. A flexible capsule surrounds the joint and supports this movement. In frozen shoulders, this capsule becomes stiff and loses flexibility.

As the capsule tightens, shoulder movement becomes limited. The patient may first feel pain during movement. Later, the shoulder becomes stiff and the arm may not move fully, even with effort. In many cases, both active and passive movement become restricted. This means the patient cannot move the shoulder properly, and even when a doctor or physiotherapist tries to move it, the range remains limited.

Frozen shoulders are not the same as normal shoulder pain. It needs proper diagnosis because symptoms may sometimes look similar to rotator cuff injury, shoulder arthritis, impingement, tendon inflammation, or neck-related nerve pain. Common Symptoms of Frozen Shoulder

Frozen shoulder symptoms usually develop slowly. Pain may begin first, followed by stiffness. In some patients, stiffness becomes the main problem over time.

Common symptoms include:

  • Shoulder pain while moving the arm
  • Stiffness in the shoulder joint
  • Difficulty lifting the arm overhead
  • Pain while reaching behind the back
  • Trouble wearing clothes
  • Difficulty combing hair
  • Shoulder pain at night
  • Pain while sleeping on the affected side
  • Reduced shoulder flexibility
  • Difficulty driving or doing household work
  • Feeling that the shoulder is locked
  • Gradual loss of shoulder movement

Many patients visit the doctor only when daily activities become difficult. However, early consultation can help reduce pain and prevent the shoulder from becoming more stiff. Stages of Frozen Shoulder

Frozen shoulder usually progresses in three stages. Understanding these stages helps patients know why symptoms change over time.

1. Freezing Stage

This is the painful stage. Shoulder pain gradually increases and movement starts reducing. Pain may become worse at night and may disturb sleep. Patients often avoid moving the arm because of pain, but reduced movement can increase stiffness.

In this stage, early diagnosis is useful. Dr. Mir Jawad Zar Khan may recommend medicines, activity changes, physiotherapy, and shoulder exercises depending on the patient's symptoms and medical condition.

2. Frozen Stage

In this stage, pain may reduce slightly, but stiffness becomes more severe. The shoulder feels tight and movement becomes limited. Activities such as wearing clothes, bathing, reaching shelves, lifting bags, or driving can become difficult.

Many patients consult an orthopedic doctor during this stage because movement becomes clearly restricted. Proper treatment during this stage focuses on improving mobility and reducing stiffness safely.

3. Thawing Stage

In the thawing stage, shoulder movement slowly starts improving. Pain reduces and flexibility gradually returns. However, recovery can take time. Guided physiotherapy and regular shoulder mobility exercises are important for better recovery.

A doctor-guided plan helps patients avoid wrong exercises, over-stretching, or delayed recovery.

What Causes Frozen Shoulder?

The exact cause of the frozen shoulder is not always clear. However, certain risk factors can increase the chances of developing this condition.

Diabetes

People with diabetes have a higher risk of frozen shoulders. They may also experience more stiffness and slower recovery. If a diabetic patient develops shoulder pain or restricted movement, early orthopedic consultation is important.

Thyroid Problems

Both hypothyroidism and hyperthyroidism may be associated with shoulder stiffness. Patients with thyroid disorders should not ignore gradual shoulder movement restriction.

Shoulder Injury

A fall, accident, fracture, ligament injury, rotator cuff problem, or shoulder trauma can sometimes lead to frozen shoulders, especially if the arm is not moved properly during recovery.

Surgery or Long Immobilisation

After surgery or injury, if the shoulder remains inactive for a long period, the capsule may become tight. This can increase the risk of frozen shoulders. Guided rehabilitation is important after orthopedic injuries and surgeries.

Age Factor

Frozen shoulders are more common after the age of 40. It may affect both men and women, but it is commonly seen in middle-aged and older adults.

Treatment Options for Frozen Shoulder

Most frozen shoulder cases can be managed without surgery when treated correctly. Dr. Mir Jawad Zar Khan may suggest a treatment plan based on the patient's pain level, stiffness, age, medical condition, and daily activity needs.

1. Medicines for Pain and Inflammation

Medicines may be prescribed to reduce pain and inflammation. Pain control is important because severe pain prevents shoulder movement and exercise. Patients should avoid taking painkillers repeatedly without medical advice, especially if they have diabetes, acidity, kidney problems, blood pressure, or heart disease.

2. Physiotherapy and Shoulder Exercises

Physiotherapy is one of the most important parts of frozen shoulder treatment. The goal is to improve movement, reduce stiffness, and restore shoulder function.

Common exercises may include:

  • Pendulum exercises
  • Wall climbing exercises
  • Assisted stretching
  • Shoulder rotation exercises
  • Range-of-motion exercises
  • Gentle strengthening exercises

Exercises should be done carefully. Overstretching or forceful movement can increase pain. Dr. Jawad may guide patients on when to begin physiotherapy and what type of movement is suitable for their stage of frozen shoulder.

3. Heat and Ice Therapy

Heat may help relax tight muscles before exercise. Ice may help reduce pain after movement or therapy. The use of heat or ice depends on the patient's symptoms and doctor's recommendation.

4. Steroid Injections

In some patients, steroid injections may help reduce pain and inflammation, especially in the painful early stage. This can make physiotherapy easier and improve shoulder movement. Injections should only be taken after proper orthopedic evaluation.

5. Hydrodilatation

Hydrodilatation is a procedure where fluid is injected into the shoulder joint to stretch the tight capsule. It may be recommended for selected patients with significant stiffness who are not improving with routine treatment.

6. Advanced Shoulder Procedures

Surgery is rarely needed for frozen shoulders. However, if stiffness and pain continue despite medicines, physiotherapy, and injections, advanced treatment may be considered. In selected cases, shoulder manipulation under anesthesia or arthroscopic capsular release may help improve movement.

Frequently Asked Questions: Frozen Shoulder Syndrome in Hyderabad


What is frozen shoulder syndrome?

Frozen shoulder syndrome, also called adhesive capsulitis, is a condition where the shoulder joint becomes painful, stiff, and difficult to move. It usually develops gradually and may affect daily activities like wearing clothes, reaching overhead, combing hair, or sleeping on the affected side.

What are the common symptoms of frozen shoulder?

Common symptoms of frozen shoulder include shoulder pain, stiffness, reduced movement, pain at night, difficulty lifting the arm, trouble reaching behind the back, and discomfort while doing routine activities. The stiffness may increase slowly over weeks or months.

What causes frozen shoulder?

Frozen shoulder can develop when the capsule around the shoulder joint becomes tight and inflamed. It may be linked with diabetes, thyroid problems, shoulder injury, surgery, long-term shoulder immobility, or age-related stiffness. In some cases, there may be no clear cause.

Can frozen shoulder be treated without surgery?

Yes, many frozen shoulder cases improve with non-surgical treatment such as medicines, physiotherapy, shoulder mobility exercises, heat or ice therapy, and injections when needed. Surgery or arthroscopic release is usually considered only when stiffness and pain do not improve with conservative treatment.
